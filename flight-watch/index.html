Project 06 · Checks in daily

Flight price monitoring.

Trip ideas written as wishes ("somewhere sunny in spring, under €300") parked in a spreadsheet. Each one is checked on its own days, and fares are judged against that row's budget and rules. "Pretty good" does not qualify.

CadenceDaily
Bookings madeZero, ever
BudgetSet per row

How it works

The setup, minus the spreadsheet itself: the briefs stay private.

  1. The brief.One row per trip idea: where from, where to (or just a mood, like "beach weather"), a date window, a ceiling price and which days of the week to check.
  2. The search.Live fares, with the dates flexed inside the window and open-jaw or mixed-carrier combinations considered. A row can also ask for airline sales generally: real price drops from a route's normal fare, not points promotions.
  3. The bar.Each row's ceiling price and flexibility set the bar. Fares are checked against it honestly, with the awkward bits, like a middle-of-the-night departure or a backpack-only allowance, stated rather than hidden.
  4. The report.Options that clear the bar, with dates and the standing caveat that fares move fast. Where the news goes is up to the row.

House rules

  • Watches and reports only. It never books, never holds, never buys.
  • Currency conversions are labelled approximate; the airline's own figure always wins.
  • Unverifiable bargains are reported as unverifiable, not as finds.
  • The trip briefs are private. This page describes the machine, not the holidays.
