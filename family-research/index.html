Family research.

A family tree with footnotes. Names, dates and family stories go into a spreadsheet; what comes back is documented history: a findings file per person, copies of the sources, and the archive letters needed to chase the rest.

How it works

The setup, minus the working files: the tree and its documents stay private.

  1. The sheet.One row per ancestor or family story: grandparents within living memory, tea planters in Ceylon and Darjeeling, a surname traced back to medieval England, a line that may reach the Mayflower.
  2. The digging.Census records, parish registers, newspaper archives, library catalogues and genealogy platforms. Every claim is tied to a source, and family lore is labelled as lore until one confirms it.
  3. The file.Each person gets a findings document and a folder of source copies, linked from their row, so the evidence is never more than a click from the conclusion.
  4. The outreach.When a record sits in an archive, the enquiry letters are drafted, a human sends them, the replies are logged and chased, and each answer starts the next round.

House rules

  • The dead and the long dead are fair game; living people stay out of the research and off this page.
  • No source, no claim. "We have always said…" is a lead, not a fact.
  • Costs are flagged before they are incurred. Archive fees get a human's yes first.
  • The working files are not linked here. This page is the story of the setup, not the files themselves.
