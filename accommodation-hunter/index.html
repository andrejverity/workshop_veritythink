Accommodation Hunter.

Looking for a place to rent or buy, written as a brief in a spreadsheet. Each hunt carries its own area, budget and must-haves, and what turns up is logged with the doubts left in.

How the sheet is laid out

The structure, minus the spreadsheet itself: the briefs and findings stay private.

  1. One control row per hunt.Rent or buy, the area and how far out to look, the budget with its currency and any extra costs to count, the must-haves, the nice-to-haves, whether the hunt is active, and how often to check and in which time zone.
  2. One findings tab per hunt.Dates sit in the leftmost column and the newest finding sits on top. A listing already seen is not logged twice. If its price or details change, the change is recorded.
  3. A guide tab.It explains the columns, so the sheet can be handed to someone else and still make sense.

How a check works

  1. Read the brief.The row sets the area, the budget and the requirements. Anything vague, like "reasonable commute", is flagged for a decision rather than guessed.
  2. Search the listing sites.Signed in with a dedicated account where a site requires it, filtering first, then opening the actual listings and reading the details.
  3. Test each listing against the row.Every requirement is marked as met, not met, or not stated. Photos and ad copy are treated as claims. Local room labels are checked against the listing rather than treated as interchangeable with bedroom counts.
  4. Log and report.Findings go into the tab, then an email summarises them for the owner of the hunt, with a link back to the sheet kept in that private email only.

Where it stands

The setup has had a first trial run: it got into a listing site, searched, read full listings and logged what it found. That hunt was then paused. No schedule is active, so nothing is being checked in the background. Turning on regular checks is a setting on a row (days, time of day, time zone), not new machinery.

House rules

  • Looks and reports only. It does not contact owners or agents, apply, or pay for anything.
  • Claims such as earthquake-resistant, sunny or quiet are reported as the listing's claim, never as verified fact, unless a document backs them.
  • Missing information is stated as missing, not filled in.
  • Logins, search areas, budgets and findings are private. This page describes the machine, not the hunt.
