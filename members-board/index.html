    How it works

    1. Anyone can readThe notes load from a spreadsheet through a small Google Apps Script web app. No sign-in is needed, and the public view never includes anyone's email address.
    2. Sign in with Google to editThe page gets a signed token from Google that says who you are. Nothing is stored in the browser after you close the tab.
    3. The go-between checks every editIt verifies the token with Google, then looks you up in the Allowlist tab of a private sheet. Editors can add and remove notes. Anyone else gets a refusal and nothing changes.
    4. One spreadsheet holds everythingThe notes are one tab and the allow list is another. Only the notes tab is ever served to the public.

    What this protects, and what it does not

    • Edits are checked on the data side, on every request. Hiding or showing buttons in this page is only cosmetic.
    • The allow list is never sent to the browser. Editors' email addresses are not shown to the public.
    • Writing needs the editor role. A signed-in stranger can read, like everyone else, but cannot change anything.
    • The notes are public. Do not put anything private in them.
    • Anyone can see this page and the go-between's address. The address is not a secret, so the edit checks are the protection.
    • Whoever can edit the Allowlist tab can approve editors. That tab is locked to named editors in the sheet.
    • It suits a handful of trusted editors and small data. Apps Script is slow (a second or two) and has daily quotas.
    Privacy · what this page does with your Google sign-in

    Anyone can read the notes without signing in. Signing in with Google is only for people who want to edit. It gives this page your email address, name and profile picture and nothing else from your Google account. Your email address is checked against a private allow list in a Google Sheet. If you sign in without being approved, your email may be added there as "pending" with no edit rights. Notes show a first name only, never an email address. Nothing is sold or shared, and only Andrej Verity and the assistant that maintains the site can see the allow list.
