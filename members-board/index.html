    How it works

    1. Sign in with GoogleThe page gets a signed token from Google that says who you are. Nothing is stored in the browser after you close the tab.
    2. The go-between checks itA small Google Apps Script web app verifies the token with Google, then looks you up in the Allowlist tab of a private spreadsheet.
    3. Only listed people get dataViewers can read. Editors can read, add and remove notes. Anyone else gets a refusal and no data.
    4. One spreadsheet holds everythingThe notes and the allow list are two tabs of the same sheet. Adding a person is adding a row.

    What this protects, and what it does not

    • The data is not in the page or the repository. Opening the page source shows no notes and no names.
    • The check happens on the data side. Hiding or showing buttons in this page is only cosmetic.
    • Writing needs the editor role, checked again by the go-between on every request.
    • Anyone can see this page and the go-between's address. The address is not a secret, so the checks are the protection.
    • An approved person can copy what they can see. Sign-in controls who gets in, not what they do with it.
    • Whoever can edit the Allowlist tab can approve people. That tab is locked to named editors in the sheet.
    • It suits a handful of trusted people and small data. Apps Script is slow (a second or two) and has daily quotas.
